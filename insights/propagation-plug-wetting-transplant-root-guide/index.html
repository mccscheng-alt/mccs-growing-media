Propagation Plugs: From Wetting to Post-Transplant Roots

MCCS Growing Media · October 14, 2026

A propagation plug needs to work through more than one stage: first wetting, tray handling, root development and establishment in the next container. This guide connects factory media with a practical buyer evaluation, without treating a selected plant as a complete trial.

Keep physical observations separate from chemical results

Wetting and roots do not measure EC or pH. State the extraction ratio, water quality, calibration, units and sample reference with each chemical result. A raw-coir specification cannot be substituted for the finished plug or the buyer's irrigation water.

Buyer questions

Do the videos guarantee a wetting time?

No. Repeat the check with your sample, starting moisture and water-application method. Edited demonstration time is not a universal specification.

Does visible vapor measure porosity?

No. Quantitative porosity or airflow requires a defined test method, sample conditioning and reported units.

Do side-by-side photos prove faster nursery production?

No. A timing or survival claim requires the full crop record, a comparable control, sample counts and agreed endpoints.

What should I send to arrange a useful sample?

Send crop and propagation method, tray information, current medium, irrigation routine, destination and trial objective. MCCS can then discuss a sample shortlist and shipping.
