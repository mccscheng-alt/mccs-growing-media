Strawberry Runner Propagation in Molded Plugs: A 13-Day Grower Record

An anonymized grower record of strawberry runner daughter plants in molded substrate plugs, bag-based moisture management and transplant assessment.

What was recorded

A grower reported starting strawberry runner daughter plants on November 3 and reporting back on November 16, 2026: 13 elapsed days. The grower described rooting as good and supplied four photographs. Some runners remain visibly connected in the photographs; the timing of separation from mother plants was not documented. This is one grower observation, not a controlled trial or a measured rooting-rate comparison.

How the grower used the plugs

The grower positioned each daughter plant against a molded plug and used wooden pins for support. Reusable plastic pins were mentioned as another option; toothpicks supported the bag and mounting arrangement. A clear resealable bag held moisture around the plug, with foliage outside. The grower reported initially placing approximately one-quarter of a bag of water inside rather than watering each plug separately. Bag volume, water quality, replenishment and measured water dose were not supplied. This description is a record of the method used, not a standard irrigation dose or an instruction to seal the whole plant.

What the photographs show

The images show the plug body, attachment arrangement and local root initials or short roots near the crown. They do not show a complete extracted root ball, measured root mass, survival percentage or yield. The grower reported fast wetting and good moisture retention, while still assessing how completely roots would bind the plug. Labels have been covered and identifying metadata removed; root structures have not been retouched.

When to assess transplant readiness

The grower suggested judging readiness around 10 to 15 days according to actual root development. This experience range is not a guaranteed transplant schedule. Check healthy new roots, plant condition and whether the plug can be handled as an intact unit; then assess a small transplant batch. Prepare a planting opening matching the actual root ball and keep the crown above the substrate surface. Record establishment after transplanting before adopting a production schedule.

Adapting the method for Saudi greenhouses

Run a small comparison with your current propagation medium using the same cultivar and comparable daughter plants. Record greenhouse temperature and humidity, water and nutrient-solution EC/pH, plug batch, whether runners are attached, water additions, rooting dates and handling losses. Avoid crown submersion, stagnant water and overheating around the bags. The grower noted that strawberry production commonly uses drip irrigation; this particular bag method was not a drip-irrigation trial. Do not copy the bag water fraction as a greenhouse irrigation recipe.

Hygiene and salinity remain batch decisions

The grower said no additional disinfection or desalination was performed during this use. That does not establish that treatment is unnecessary, or describe processing before supply. Check batch specifications, water quality and the nursery hygiene programme. Cultivar, sample count, exact plug size and formulation, batch EC/pH, environmental records and post-transplant outcomes remain unreported.
